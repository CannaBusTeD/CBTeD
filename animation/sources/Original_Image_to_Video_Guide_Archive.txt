2. The Core Law of Image-to-Video Prompting

   The Redundancy Paradox: If an image has a vintage microphone and neon sign, writing "A vintage chrome microphone
   standing in front of a blue neon bar sign" wastes prompt attention tokens and triggers AI hallucinations (unwanted morphing,
   duplicate props, melting hands).


Instead, your prompt formula should consist purely of four kinematic vectors:

 1. Kinematic Action (Subject Motion): Micro-expressions, postural weight shifts, physical gestures, head turns.
 2. Camera Trajectory: Precise lens behavior, tracking paths, focal push/pull, orbital speed.
 3. Atmospheric Dynamics: Volumetric lighting shifts, smoke dissipation, wind interacting with fabric/hair, shadow drift.
 4. Acoustic Foley & Ambience: Synchronized sound effects, room tone, environmental textures.

4. Multi-Clip Extension Architecture (0s → 10s → 20s → 30s)
When extending generations in Grok Imagine, continuity errors occur if the next prompt contradicts the momentum of the
previous frame. Follow this 3-tier structure:

  • Clip 1 (0–10s) — The Establishing Dynamic: Introduces subtle baseline motion and establishes the environmental
    pace.
  • Clip 2 (10–20s) — The Dynamic Shift: Progresses naturally from the exact finishing posture of Clip 1. Incorporates
    secondary camera rotation or gesture escalation.
  • Clip 3 (20–30s) — The Climax / Resolution: Completes the visual arc, settling into a deliberate final pose or slow
    camera pull-back.
